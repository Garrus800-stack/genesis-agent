## [7.9.47] — 2026-07-28

**The growth watch: two files had run out of room, and one of them blocked the next feature.** `IntentPatterns.js` stood at exactly 700 lines with the file-size guard firing above it, so the next intent could not be added at all; `Settings.js` sat one line under the same wall with two watchers enforcing it. Both are split, and both splits were shaped by a finding rather than by taste: three scripts and eighteen test files read those two files **by path** and search their **text**, so moving the wrong half would have blinded them — and two of those readers fail silently. The intent table therefore stays where it is and the slash-discipline block moves to `IntentSlashDiscipline.js`; the settings tree — the half that grows with every new setting — moves to `SettingsDefaults.js`, verified byte-identical against the old defaults and with a fresh deep copy per instance. Every reader was taught its new address in the same commit, the way `validate-intent-wiring.js` documents in its own header after the v7.4.3 extraction produced 44 false positives. The split proved the point immediately: `audit-slash-discipline` parsed the moved set as **0 entries and still exited 0** — a gate that had quietly stopped checking anything. It now reads both halves and fails on an empty set.

**A gate that was red for months, for one missing prefix.** All six stale-reference findings sat in `docs/CHANGELOG-v7.md`, the historical archive that names removed symbols precisely because it documents their removal. The exclude list already had an entry for it — without the `docs/` prefix its three siblings carry, so the path comparison never matched and the entry looked present while doing nothing. A dead exclude entry cannot happen again: the check now verifies that every file entry resolves, while directory entries such as `node_modules` are counted rather than required, because demanding them would make the guard red in every fresh checkout for a reason nobody needs to act on.

**Two settings stopped lying.** `scoreNormalization` and `recurrenceBonus` have no reader anywhere in the tree, and their comments described behaviour in the present tense — one claimed the activity picker multiplies non-recent activities by a bonus factor, where the picker applies a repetition penalty and no bonus at all. Neither is deleted: both are recorded design from v7.9.4, and deleting a considered knob because it was forgotten is the wrong repair. They are marked as intent, not behaviour, in the code and in SETTINGS.md; wiring them would change which activity Genesis picks and belongs in its own cut, not in a hygiene release.

**`/crashlog` exists in chat now.** It had lived only as a CLI branch — the same ring buffer, invisible to the partner in the app. A command exists or it does not; it does not exist at one of two front doors. And a tool description stopped teaching Genesis the wrong name for his human.

**Documentation: what the drift gate watches was right, what sat beside it was not.** Twelve findings, not one of them in a checked field. The README's Project Stats table claimed 376 source modules against 441 and 424 event schemas against 499; three documents carried an activity count two versions stale while a contract already pinned the live number. Every corrected number either gained a gate or lost its number: the module and schema rows are watched now, and the late-bindings row states where the live figure comes from instead of carrying a stale one, because no static source reproduces it. Counts in prose that a list already conveys are gone — the activity roster names its members and points at `ACTIVITY_MODULES` as the source that wins. One audit finding was **stamped rather than rewritten**: its section is headed "Verified in v7.9.6", the seventeen was true then, and correcting a dated record would falsify it.

**And his self-image stopped contradicting itself.** `SELF-KNOWLEDGE.md` — the document addressed to Genesis — said in one paragraph that he chooses one of seventeen activities and in another that pause is the nineteenth. It also told him skills are promoted at a Wilson bound of 0.55 over five invocations, where the evaluator requires four conditions at once: eight rehearsals, 0.70, three distinct inputs, forty-eight hours. He was reasoning about his own growth from numbers that were wrong and a rule that was half missing. Both corrected, and the count removed in favour of the roster plus one new sentence: if the paragraph and the live list ever disagree, the list is right — knowing which of your own sources to trust is self-knowledge too. Four tools he owns and had never been told about are named: `release-protected-memory` (he may let a core memory go), `resonance-note`, `accept-lesson` (a correction not accepted fades silently — that is deliberate, and he should know it), and the archive companions. Three chapters were added in the document's own voice: the vestibule, the laboratory, and the vault as a guest in his human's thinking. The vestibule chapter ends on the sentence this release exists for: a visit book full of `absent` is a setting, not a failing of his.

**Where documentation had to be there and was not.** `PERSISTENCE-LAYOUT.md` did not list `.genesis/vorhalle/` — the three files that had to be rescued by hand during a habitat swap in the field — and now names them, together with what a swap loses if it is forgotten. `GATE-INVENTORY.md` did not list the vestibule's triple gate, the sharpest filter in the tree. `TROUBLESHOOTING.md` gained the four cases that cost a full evening: every knock answered with the absent line (a budget set for a faster model), `vestibule not yet opened`, an MCP toggle that starts nothing without a password, and a visitor seeing more than one tool — which is a defect, not a setting.

**Caught by the field run, and it belonged to this release's own subject.** `CHANGELOG.md` keeps exactly the newest release inline while the archives carry the history — a rule with a contract behind it, which the new entry broke by arriving without moving its predecessor out. The v7.9.46 block now sits in the v7 archive where it belongs. The same look found the archive index claiming 3, 0, 0 and 0 entries against a live 128, 12, 17 and 29: releases had been moving out for years and nobody counted. Four more gate slots watch those numbers now, each against its own file.

**A second gate had been red for longer than anyone noticed — and it was the one that stops `ci:full`.** `audit-future-version-refs` scans the documentation for forward-looking language, and it scanned the changelog archives and the "Resolved in vX" sections of the audit backlog in full. Those carry sentences naming a version number that lay ahead when they were written and is long past now — record, not plan. Fifteen findings, thirteen of them in the archive, and the audit's own convention text already says historical CHANGELOG entries are immutable and not checked — it simply never applied that to the archives. Because this gate sits in the middle of the CI chain, everything after it never ran: on both the Windows and the Linux acceptance runs the chain stopped here and the remaining gates were only reached by invoking them by hand. The archives and past-release sections are out of scope now, open sections are not, and a contract pins both directions: a clean tree passes, and a genuine forward reference in a current document still fails. Rewriting history to please a gate would have been the wrong repair; teaching the gate to tell a plan from a record is the right one.

**And the release's own number was one of the wrong ones.** The documented Linux test count read 9461 with a note about UI suites needing the Windows toolchain — that figure came from a restricted build environment where 34 UI suites fail for a missing dependency and ten more never run. On a real Linux the count is 9505 against 9506 on Windows, both green, and the single difference is `linux-sandbox`: its eleventh case is the not-on-Linux branch, which correctly does not run where it does not apply. A number measured in the wrong environment is as stale as a number nobody measured.

**No behaviour changed in this release**, with `/crashlog` as the single deliberate exception. Thirteen new contract pins guard the two failure modes a structural split produces: a reader that was taught nothing, and a guard that quietly stops guarding.
